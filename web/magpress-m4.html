How it makes a magnet

Each magnet takes two strokes of the handle. The die table slides between two stations. Each lower die is loaded while it is outside the press, so your fingers are never under the ram. The upper die stays on the ram and does both jobs. This is the slide-type layout from the photo you sent. The two-stroke die action follows the badge-press patents listed in section 12, all expired.

    Layer stack, front to back

    Mylar film (0.075 mm PET) → printed photo (120–150 g/m² matte paper) → tinplate shell (0.20–0.23 mm, drawn tray with clipped corners) → back plate (1.0–1.5 mm ABS/acrylic, or cardboard) → adhesive magnet sheet, applied after pressing.

    Sellers ship slightly different stacks (cardboard backs, acrylic backs, tin backs). The die set and the consumables must match, so buy consumables to this pack's sizes or adjust the die values in section 05.

    Why these choices
    • Toggle, not cam: force rises as the stroke closes, which is where crimping needs it. The stroke ends 8° before dead centre, so it can't lock over.
    • H-frame with two columns: it is symmetric, so the ram can't tilt under load the way a C-frame does.
    • Slide, not turntable: it is simpler to make. Stop blocks give repeatable positions, and the table can't drift while you press.
    • Grip lands over the base tongue: the press can't tip toward you at the end of the stroke.
    • Keyed die shoes: the A and B shoes have different dowel patterns, so they can't go on the wrong station.

    Force through the stroke

    The handle drives a toggle. The crank (O→A, 40 mm) and the link (A→B, 60 mm) straighten as the handle comes down. Ram movement per degree of handle travel shrinks toward the bottom, so the force multiplies, reaching about ×31 at the end. Mechanical advantage is MA = L / (dy/dφ) with dy/dφ = a·sinφ + a²·sinφ·cosφ / √(b² − a²sin²φ), where L = 290 mm (pivot to grip centre), a = 40, b = 60, and φ is the crank angle from straight down. The chart assumes 200 N on the grip and 90% efficiency through the three pins.

    The required force for each die set is an estimate: about 7 N per mm of magnet edge for the crimp spring preload, scaled from the round-badge spring figures in US 4,696,086, then × 2.2 for the final crimp. Measure it on the prototype with a load cell (section 10). Stroke 1 needs much less force and stops at about 35.5 mm, when the plug meets the shell.

    05

    Swappable die sets

    Common interface, same on all four sets
    • Upper die: Ø16 g6 shank with a V-groove, fitted into the Ø16 H7 die holder and locked by one M5 cone-point screw. A Ø4 pin in the die top fixes the rotation, which matters for rectangles. A shim pack on each upper die sets its depth once, so swapping needs no adjustment.
    • Lower dies: each sits on a 140 × 124 × 10 shoe with two Ø8 dowels and two M5 thumb screws. Shoe A's dowels are 90 mm apart at the back; shoe B's are 60 mm apart at the front.
    • Marking: shoe A has a green band and is laser-marked "A · PICKUP". Shoe B has a red band and "B · CRIMP". Each part carries its drawing number and size code, for example MP-207 6590 A. The long side of every rectangular die runs along the table.
    Materials
    • Production: 1.2379 / Cr12MoV (D2 type), vacuum hardened and tempered to 58–60 HRC. Wire-EDM the profiles after hardening, grind the faces, and polish the forming edges to Ra 0.2–0.4 µm. Optional TiN or hard-chrome coat.
    • Prototype: 7075-T6 aluminium, CNC machined and hard anodised. This is the cheapest way to prove the fits. Expect faster wear on the forming edges.
    • Shoes: 6061-T6 or C45. Springs: ISO 10243 die springs, colour-coded green (light) for A, blue (medium) for the upper frame, red or yellow (strong) for B.

    Fits are starting values. Upper frame opening = shell + 0.35 mm, so it grips the wrapped shell by friction. Plug = shell + 0.30 mm, sliding in the frame. Crimp frame opening = shell + 0.45 mm. Lap the upper frame opening on the first prototype until a wrapped shell lifts out of die A reliably and pushes out cleanly over die B.

    Frame and drive parts

    One frame serves all four die sets. Base, crown and ram carry the column holes at 210.00 mm pitch. Machine all three with the same program and datum scheme, or line-bore them together, so the ram slides freely once the column nuts are torqued.

    Machine-shop drawings: drawing set MP-000 to MP-209 (30 A3 sheets, PDF) with DXF profiles for cutting and wire-EDM. Where this page and the drawings differ, the drawings govern.

    Bill of materials

    Budget guide (rough, 2026)
    • Frame and drive parts, one prototype from a local CNC shop: about $600–1,200.
    • Bought parts (bushings, springs, pins, fasteners, grip, feet): about $80–150.
    • Die set in 7075 aluminium: about $120–300 each. In hardened Cr12MoV: about $250–600 each.
    • Batch pricing (10+) and shops in India or China usually cost much less. Get three quotes from this drawing pack.
    Make or buy

    Make the frame, drive and dies; they are simple prismatic parts that any 3-axis CNC shop and wire-EDM shop can handle. Buy everything that is a catalogue item: bushings, die springs, pins, fasteners, ball plungers, thumb screws, grip and feet. Buy consumables (shells, film, backs, magnet sheet) from a magnet-supplies vendor in the four sizes.

    Assembly

    Each step has a "Show in 3D" button that opens the build view at that step

      Swapping a die set

      1. Rest the handle fully up on its stop and slide the table so both stations are clear.
      2. Upper die: support it with one hand, loosen the M6 side screw on the die holder, and pull the die straight down. Push in the new die with the flat facing the screw and the pin in its slot, then tighten.
      3. Lower dies: loosen the two thumb screws on each shoe and lift the shoe off its dowels. Fit the new A shoe (green) on the left station and the new B shoe (red) on the right. The dowels prevent mix-ups.
      4. Make one slow dry stroke on each station with no parts. The upper frame's lead-in should enter both lower frames without touching.
      5. Make two test magnets and check the corners and the crimp before running a batch.

Wear cut-resistant gloves when handling loose tinplate shells. The press weighs about 28 kg, so use two people to lift it.

      Before you sell it

      The two-stroke badge-die principle is described in patents that have now expired (US 4,696,086, US 6,038,944, US 6,938,518). This pack uses its own frame and die geometry rather than copying a particular vendor's machine. Before selling in your market, have a patent agent search for current patents and registered designs on square magnet presses, and give the product its own look and name.

      Research and references

      No open-source CAD for this kind of press turned up. The design is built from the expired patents below, published operating guides and vendor specs. Vendor pages are marketing claims, so they were used only for sizes and part lists.

      • US 4,696,086 — Die set apparatus for badges (Dickerson, 1987). Pickup and crimp die sequence, punch springs ≈ 100 lb, crimp springs ≈ 350 lb.
      • US 6,938,518 — Two-stroke machine for buttons with coplanar shell flanges (Tecre, 2005). Rectangular shells 0.008–0.009 in, clipped corners, spring cells, scalloped bevel.
      • US 6,038,944 — Apparatus for manufacturing buttons (Tecre, 2000). Box frame vs C/H frames, slide vs turntable, handle-over-base anti-tip.
      • US 4,571,806 — Apparatus for assembling buttons. Toggle-joint drive in a hand press.
      • BPL Maker button-maker guide, MacEwan button press guide, Emory TechLab handout. Operating sequence and common faults.
      • JXMide square magnet machine and the Made-in-China listing. The four standard sizes and consumable options.
      • 63.5 mm kit (press order: shell, mylar, paper, acrylic, magnet) and square kit listing (mould A / mould B steps).
      • Grawerton 65×90 set (tin, cardboard, magnet, foil) and IDGamax 78×53 supplies.
